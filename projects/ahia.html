01Overview

A multi-tenant retail platform for small traders, built for how trade actually happens: a counter sale, a market run, a customer who sends a list and pays on delivery. It ships as a backend service, a web application and an offline-first mobile client.

The engineering spine is a five-layer architecture with enforced import contracts: entities, schemas, persistence, services and transport. No outward imports, no logic in transport, no database driver outside the persistence boundary, and the build fails on a violation rather than a reviewer catching it.

02How it is built

  • Every business-owned table carries a row-level security policy bound into the transaction, so a query that forgets its tenant filter returns nothing rather than another shop's data. A missing scope fails closed.
  • Sales, payments, stock movements, expenses, audit events and the ledger are append-only and committed together, so a failure part-way through cannot leave half-recorded money. The rule is enforced by database triggers, not by convention.
  • Money is never floating point. A price arriving as a float is refused rather than rounded, prices carry at most two decimal places, and they travel the wire as decimal strings so no client passes money through a binary float.
  • Offline devices queue operations under a stable operation identifier and push them through a sync service, so a retry after a lost reply cannot book the same sale twice. Inventory merges additively, catalogue edits resolve last-writer-wins, and a customer draft conflict is surfaced rather than silently resolved.
  • Authorization is data. Atomic permissions compose into permission sets and then roles, resolved deny-by-default, and a caller cannot grant a permission they do not hold. The interface hides what a role cannot do instead of offering a button that fails.
  • The catalogue is an arbitrarily deep category tree with price inheritance, so a grade carries one price and only the exceptions are listed by name. A customer can add an item the shop has never stocked, leaving the price for the trader to set.
  • A customer builds a list from a public link without an account and is remembered by phone number. The list is shared as a generated image or a multi-page PDF, because a typed list arrives truncated on a phone and cannot be forwarded as a list.
  • Dispatch records the transporter, the waybill number, the trip cost and a tracking link, so a parcel on a road is attributable and the cost of the run comes off what the list made.
  • Storage and media sit behind a provider-neutral port with two interchangeable adapters, server-side validation and optimisation, and per-tenant quota accounting that holds under concurrent uploads.
  • Deployment is one push: the image is built in CI and addressed by commit, a migration job runs before traffic moves, and a rollback is a pointer at an earlier tag rather than a rebuild.
  • Roughly two thousand tests, including architecture contracts, an ASCII guard, secret scanning and a dependency audit gate that fails the build on a critical finding.
